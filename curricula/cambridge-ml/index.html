Theoretical Foundations

University of Cambridge: Mathematics of Machine Learning

Bridging Cambridge's theoretical statistical learning theory with high-dimensional $p \gg n$ cancer genomics, transcriptomics, and biological sequence modeling.

Lecture 03: Bias-Variance Decomposition & K-Fold Cross-Validation

Decomposes expected generalization error under squared loss into irreducible noise $\sigma^2$, estimation variance $\text{Var}(\hat{f}(x))$, and model bias $\text{Bias}^2(\hat{f}(x))$. Explores optimal hyperparameter selection via $k$-fold cross-validation in small sample size biological cohorts.

Lecture 09: Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS) & Representer Theorem

Infinite-dimensional function spaces and the Kimeldorf-Wahba Representer Theorem. Proves that any regularized empirical risk minimizer in an RKHS admits a finite expansion $\hat{f}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i K(x, x_i)$, forming the basis for non-linear genomic support vector machines and Gaussian processes.

Lecture 12: High-Dimensional Sparsity: L1 (Lasso) vs. L2 (Ridge) Regularization

Geometric and analytical proofs of sparsity induction via singular corners of the $\ell_1$ norm unit ball. Essential for biomarker discovery where candidate genes ($p \approx 20,000$) vastly exceed patient sample counts ($n \approx 100$).

Lecture 15–16: AdaBoost, Functional Gradient Descent & Lasso Equivalence

Exponential loss minimization and reweighting dynamics. Features the landmark proof showing that infinitesimal forward stagewise boosting with small learning rate $\epsilon \to 0$ traces the identical continuous regularization trajectory as the Lasso.
